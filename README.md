Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (CS:GO) is a tactical first-person shooter developed by Valve and Hidden Path Entertainment. Released in 2012, it is the fourth main installment in the Counter-Strike series, succeeding Counter-Strike: Source. CS:GO pits two opposing teams—Terrorists and Counter-Terrorists—against each other in objective-based rounds. The core gameplay revolves around two primary modes: Bomb Defusal: Terrorists must plant a bomb at a designated site and defend it until detonation; Counter-Terrorists aim to prevent the plant or defuse the bomb. Hostage Rescue: Counter-Terrorists must rescue hostages held by Terrorists and escort them to a rescue zone. Matches are round-based (up to 30 rounds in competitive), with players purchasing weapons, armor, and utility equipment at the start of each round using money earned from kills, objective completions, and round wins. Economy management is a key strategic layer. CS:GO is renowned for its high skill ceiling, emphasizing precise aim, recoil control, map knowledge, sound awareness, and team coordination. Movement mechanics like counter-strafing and crouch-jumping add depth. The game features iconic weapons (AK-47, M4A4, AWP), and a robust skin economy where cosmetic weapon finishes can be traded or sold for real value. Competitive play is structured via a ranking system (Skill Groups from Silver to Global Elite). It also includes casual, deathmatch, and wingman (2v2) modes. Beyond official matchmaking, a thriving community runs custom servers, mods (e.g., Surf, KZ), and third-party platforms like ESEA and FaceIt. CS:GO became a cornerstone of esports, hosting major tournaments like the annual Majors (e.g., PGL, ESL One, IEM). Its legacy continued until 2023, when it was succeeded by *Counter-Strike 2* (CS2), which upgraded the engine to Source 2 while retaining CS:GO’s core mechanics. However, CS:GO remains a landmark title for defining modern tactical shooters.
